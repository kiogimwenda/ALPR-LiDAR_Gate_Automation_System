1. Introduction & Board Overview

This guide walks you through designing a custom PCB for the gate automation field controller using KiCad 10.0. The board replaces the breadboard prototype and integrates all gate-side electronics into a single, production-ready, 4-layer PCB sized to fit inside a standard IP65 enclosure.

Revision 2.0 (July 2026). This revision supersedes the April 2026 KiCad 9.0 guide. Two things changed: the tooling moved to KiCad 10.0, and — far more important — the pin map was re-synchronized with the shipped firmware (v1.0.0). Revision 1.0 predated the firmware's driver work (Phases 4.4–4.5) and disagreed with it on the entire W5500 SPI bus, the W5500 INT/RST lines, and the status LEDs; it also placed RS-485 on pins the firmware later took for LEDs, and double-booked IO21. A board routed from revision 1.0 will not run the firmware. §1.4 is now the single source of truth for every net; every schematic section and Appendix A have been corrected against it.
Revision 2.1 (July 2026). Caught during schematic review of the first built power-supply sheet. Three fixes to §4.3 / §5.1 / Appendix B, all on the 5V buck rail: (1) the VSENSE feedback divider's R_top was wrong — 43 kΩ paired with the 10 kΩ bottom leg actually sets ~6.47V, not the claimed 5.0V; corrected to 30.9 kΩ. (2) the power-path diagram and BOM were missing two components that §4.3's own tables required but never drew: the series reverse-polarity diode ahead of the TVS, and the PH-to-GND catch/freewheeling diode the TPS5430 needs (it has no internal low-side switch) — both are now in the diagram and BOM. (3) the "C_comp / R_comp" compensation pair in §4.3 has been removed — the TPS5430 has no external COMP pin; compensation is fixed internally, and those two components never did anything.

1.1 What This Board Does

  • Runs the ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8 module as the main controller
  • Provides wired Ethernet (W5500 + RJ45) for communication with the GPU server
  • Drives up to 4 relay outputs (gate motor trigger, leaf 2, spare 1, spare 2)
  • Reads 3 digital inputs (limit switch OPEN, limit switch CLOSED, safety beam)
  • Includes RS-485 transceiver for future CENTURION motor direct-drive
  • Accepts 12V DC input, regulates to 5V and 3.3V on-board
  • USB-C port for firmware upload and debug console
  • Status LEDs for power, network link, authorization, and fault

1.3 Design Philosophy

  • Reliability first: Generous trace widths, proper decoupling, TVS protection on all external I/O
  • Hand-solderable: No components smaller than 0603 (1608 metric). QFP/QFN packages have 0.5mm+ pitch
  • Testable: Test points on all power rails and critical signals
  • Expandable: Two spare relay channels + unpopulated headers for future use

Why This Footprint

  • The module is soldered directly to the PCB via castellated half-hole pads — no pin headers needed
  • Much more compact and reliable than mounting a DevKit board
  • The center GND pad provides excellent thermal and electrical grounding
  • KiCad 10.0's official footprint matches Espressif's recommended land pattern exactly

Critical Layout Rules

  • Antenna keepout: No copper (no traces, no ground fill, no vias) within 15 mm of the antenna end of the module. The antenna is at the end opposite the castellated pads.
  • Ground plane: Connect the center pad to the GND plane with at least 9 thermal-relief vias (0.3 mm drill, 0.6 mm pad)
  • Board edge: The antenna end should extend to or overhang the board edge for best RF performance. If WiFi/BLE is needed, place the module at a board corner.

USB-C Circuit

  • CC1 and CC2 pins: Each pulled down to GND via 5.1 kΩ resistor (signals "device" role to host)
  • VBUS: Connected through a Schottky diode (SS14) to prevent back-feeding when external 12V is also connected
  • D+ and D-: Connected to ESP32-S3's USB pins (IO19=D-, IO20=D+) via 22Ω series resistors

Why This Footprint

  • The GCT USB4105 is the most popular USB-C 2.0 receptacle in JLCPCB's parts library
  • Mid-mount style: the connector body is level with the board surface — flush mounting in an enclosure
  • 16-pin (not 24-pin) because we only need USB 2.0 data — simpler routing, fewer pads
  • Through-hole anchor tabs provide mechanical strength for the cable insertion force

Why Through-Hole Relays

  • Gate motors draw high surge current. The SRD relay's 10A contacts handle the inductive kick from motor contactors without welding.
  • Through-hole relays are physically robust — they won't crack off the board under vibration from a sliding gate motor.
  • Easily replaceable in the field (just desolder and swap) — important for a residential product.
  • The 5V coil draws ~90mA, well within our 5V rail's 3A budget.
Actuator-profile decision (bench-plan risk R4). The firmware's residential profile drives REL1/REL2 as two motion contactors, while ADR-008 specifies a single dry-contact trigger pulse into the Centurion controller. The four-channel board deliberately covers both outcomes of that decision (recorded at commissioning, §3.3 of the commissioning checklist): pulse profile uses one channel, two-contactor uses two, and the spares absorb a pedestrian-open or lock output later. No PCB change is needed either way — that's why the channel count survives the open decision.

Three polarities that are easy to get backwards — verify each in schematic ERC and again visually before layout:
  • C_boot connects BOOT to PH, not to GND. Tying it to GND removes the high-side FET's gate-drive supply — the regulator will not switch correctly.

7.4 Signal Routing

Routing Order (route in this sequence for best results)

  1. USB differential pair — most impedance-sensitive. Route first while the board is uncongested.
    • Use Route → Differential Pair in KiCad 10.0
    • Set pair gap to 0.15 mm in Net Classes
    • Route on Top layer only (F.Cu), directly over the unbroken GND plane
    • No vias on the differential pair
  2. 25 MHz crystal traces — short, symmetric, with ground guard.
    • Route on Top layer, keep traces under 5mm
    • Place ground vias around both crystal pads
  3. SPI bus (ESP32 → W5500) — parallel routing, same layer
    • Route CLK, MOSI, MISO, CS as a group on F.Cu
    • Keep traces parallel with 0.3mm minimum spacing between them
    • Maximum length: 50mm. Target: 30mm or less.
  4. Power traces — thick traces on F.Cu connecting to power plane vias
    • Buck converter SW node to inductor: 1.5mm wide, as short as possible (<5mm)
    • Use multiple vias (3–4) to connect power traces to their respective inner-layer planes
  5. GPIO signals — relay drivers, LED outputs, sensor inputs
    • Standard 0.25mm traces
    • Route relay control signals on B.Cu (bottom layer) to keep them away from the sensitive top-layer signals
  6. RS-485 bus
    • Route A and B as a differential pair (100Ω, though less critical than USB)
    • Keep traces parallel, short run to the screw terminal

Routing Tips in KiCad 10.0

  • Interactive router: Press X to start routing, V to place a via and switch layers
  • Push-and-shove: Enable in Interactive Router Settings (gear icon) — it moves existing traces out of the way as you route
  • Length tuning: After routing the USB pair, use the tuning tool to add serpentine to the shorter trace. KiCad 10 overhauled tuning (Tuning Profiles, time-domain constraints) — overkill for USB full-speed, but the profile UI is the same one you'll use.
  • Teardrops: After routing, run Edit → Apply Teardrops — adds reliability to pad-trace connections
  • Design blocks (new in 10): The relay driver channel repeats four times — place and route one channel cleanly, save it as a design block, and stamp the other three. Same for the three sensor-input protection channels.
  • Lasso selection (new in 10): Freeform area select makes grabbing one routed channel for duplication much less fiddly than rectangle-select ever was.

7.5 Antenna Keepout Zone

  1. Identify the antenna end of the ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 module (the narrow end, opposite the castellated pads)
  2. On ALL copper layers (F.Cu, In1.Cu, In2.Cu, B.Cu), create a Rule Area:
    • Click Place → Rule Area
    • Check: "Keep out copper fill", "Keep out tracks", "Keep out vias"
    • Draw a rectangle extending 15mm from the antenna end
  3. No components should be placed in this zone on any layer
  4. The board edge should be at or within this zone (antenna overhangs board edge = best performance)
If you skip this step: The WiFi/BLE range will be reduced by 50–80%. To be clear about today's posture: the shipped system needs no radio — control, telemetry, and OTA all run over the W5500 wired Ethernet (ADR-011 / ADR-009). The keepout is cheap insurance for the futures that do want the radio (WiFi as a network fallback, BLE commissioning) and good RF hygiene regardless — don't spend it on copper.

7.6 Thermal Relief & Copper Fills

  1. Top and Bottom copper fills: After all routing is complete, add GND fills on F.Cu and B.Cu:
    • Place → Zone, Net = GND, covering entire board
    • Set zone clearance to 0.3mm (slightly larger than default to avoid acid traps)
    • Set minimum island area to 5 mm² (removes useless copper fragments)
  2. Thermal relief on pads: KiCad defaults to thermal relief (spoked connections) for pads connected to copper fills. Keep this default — it makes hand soldering possible. For the ESP32 GND pad and the TPS5430 exposed pad, consider direct connection (solid) for better thermal performance.
  3. Via stitching: After fills, add via stitching to connect F.Cu GND fill to the L2 GND plane:
    • Place vias on a 5mm grid across the board (where space allows)
    • In KiCad 10.0: Edit → Fill All Zones, then use the via stitching plugin or manually place vias connected to GND

11. Assembly & Testing Checklist

11.1 Solder Order (if hand-assembling)

  1. Shortest components first: SMD resistors and capacitors (0603) using solder paste + hot air or iron with fine tip
  2. ICs: W5500 (LQFP-48) — use flux + drag-solder technique. Then MAX485 (SOIC-8), SS8050 transistors (SOT-23)
  3. Medium-height: AMS1117 (SOT-223), TPS5430 (HSOP-8), crystal, USB-C connector, LEDs
  4. Tall components last: ESP32-S3 module (castellated pads — use lots of flux, solder one corner first, align, then all pads). Electrolytic capacitor. Inductor.
  5. Through-hole last: Relays (4×), screw terminals, RJ45 jack, pin headers, mounting standoffs

11.2 First Power-On Test Sequence

  1. Visual inspection: Check for solder bridges (especially on W5500 QFP and ESP32 module). Use magnification (loupe or microscope).
  2. Continuity check: With multimeter in continuity mode:
    • Verify no short between +12V and GND
    • Verify no short between +5V and GND
    • Verify no short between +3.3V and GND
  3. Power-on (12V): Apply 12V to J_PWR. Measure:
    • TP_5V: should read 4.9–5.1V
    • TP_3V3: should read 3.25–3.35V
    • LED_PWR: should illuminate green
  4. USB test: Connect USB-C cable. Computer should enumerate a USB device (ESP32-S3).
  5. Flash test firmware: Flash the real gate firmware (cd firmware && idf.py flash monitor). Expect the boot banner and the bare-bench Faulted state described in the bench validation plan Stage 1 — on this board the status LEDs (red/green/yellow) show the fault pattern. (Unlike the DevKit, the bare WROOM module has no onboard RGB LED — rev 1.0's "RGB should blink" check was a DevKit-ism.)
  6. Ethernet test: Connect Ethernet cable. LED_NET should illuminate. Ping test to server IP.
  7. Relay test: Run GPIO toggle test. Listen for relay click. Verify continuity across NO contacts with multimeter.
  8. Input test: Short each sensor input to GND. Firmware should register the state change.
